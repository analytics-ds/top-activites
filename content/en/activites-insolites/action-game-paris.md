You came across the term while looking for an escape room with a bit more movement, and you are not quite sure what it covers. The action game is its own format, with its own rules, its own price and its own target groups. Here is how it works, what it costs, and where it clearly beats a classic escape room.

## What an action game actually is

An action game is a **course of several rooms of physical and mental challenges** that your team clears to score as many points as possible. The clearest image is an urban Fort Boyard, with dexterity, speed and logic challenges chained together in an immersive set.

The difference with an escape room is clear cut. In an escape room, the team stays in a single room and tries to get out by solving puzzles before the clock runs out. In an action game, there is **no door to unlock and no final puzzle**, the course ends when time runs out or every room has been cleared, and the score decides between teams. Our [action game definition page](/en/lexique/action-game/) covers the basics in a couple of lines if you want the short version.

That is exactly what a lot of people searching for a sporty escape room are looking for. The phrase is misleading, a sporty escape room does not really exist, it is an action game they are describing without knowing it.

## How a session actually runs

The format follows the same mechanics everywhere, whatever the provider.

**The briefing.** About ten minutes to set the rules of the course and introduce the setting, often staged to get you into the mood right from the entrance.

**The room course.** Your team moves through a series of challenges, usually between five and ten depending on the venue, mixing dexterity, speed, coordination and a bit of thinking. Each room scores points on its own.

**The final score.** On the way out, the ranking comes in, often compared with other teams who started at the same time or on the same day. That ranking replaces the "we made it out" satisfaction you get from an escape room.

Allow about an hour of play in total, briefing included, a bit more for the longer 90-minute to two-hour formats some providers offer.

## What it costs, and for what group size

| Element | What to know |
|---|---|
| Playing time | About 1h, up to 2h for longer formats |
| Team size | Usually 2 to 6 players |
| Budget per person | €20 to €40 for 1h, up to €45-50 for longer formats |
| Large groups | Staggered starts roughly every 10 minutes, up to 40, 60 players or more in one slot |
| Minimum age | Often from age 7-8 with an adult |

The price almost always follows a sliding scale, the bigger the team, the lower the price per head. That is the case at Bomb Squad, on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd arrondissement, the main action game venue right in central Paris. The format runs for one hour, in teams of 2 to 6 players, with departures every ten minutes that let up to 60 participants play across a two-hour window. The price drops from 45 euros per player for two, to 28 euros for six, and the activity is open from age 7 with an accompanying adult.

Outside central Paris, the format also exists on larger sites further out, with courses that tend to run longer but a trip to plan for. That is a good trade-off if you want a more spectacular set and do not mind the travel.

## What really differs from one provider to another

Not every venue is the same, and it is not only about the theme. Four things are worth checking before booking, rather than picking based on the photos alone.

**The number of rooms in the course.** More rooms usually means a longer session and a higher price. A short course works well for a one-hour lunch break, a long one suits an outing built as the main event of the day.

**How much is physical versus mental.** Some providers lean on speed and dexterity, others keep a good dose of escape-room-style puzzles in some rooms. If your group includes people who dislike running, it is worth asking for the course details when booking.

**Accessibility.** A multi-room course sometimes involves stairs, tight passages or dark areas, which is not always workable for someone with reduced mobility. That information is not always listed online, so a quick call before booking avoids surprises.

**Minimum age and supervision of minors.** Most venues set a minimum age around 7 or 8 with an accompanying adult, but the exact conditions vary by provider, worth checking if the group includes children or teenagers.

## Which occasion suits an action game

This is where the format really shines, because it adapts to very different needs.

**For a [bachelor party](/en/evg/) or [bachelorette party](/en/evjf/)**, an action game gets the group moving and adds friendly competition without putting anyone individually on the spot the way a [murder mystery party](/en/activites-insolites/murder-mystery-party-paris/) does. A short hour of play fits easily before or after the evening's dinner.

**For a birthday**, the format works for a group of teenagers as well as a group of adults, thanks to adjustable physical effort and teams that form naturally by friendship. To pick the venue and the package, read our guide on [where to celebrate your birthday in Paris](/en/anniversaires/where-to-celebrate-birthday-paris/).

**For a team building event**, it is one of the easiest formats to scale for a large headcount. Staggered starts in teams of 2 to 6 absorb groups of 40 to 60 people without splitting them across different experiences the way a classic escape room does, where each room caps the number of players. Our guide to [organising a team building event](/en/team-building-seminaires/team-building-tips/) breaks down budget tiers by team size.

## Action game, escape room or murder mystery, how to decide

The three formats look alike from a distance and share almost nothing up close.

| Format | What matters | Group | Individual exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action game | Speed, dexterity, thinking as a team | 2 to 6, scalable with multiple teams | Low, you play as a team |
| Escape room | Thinking and cooperation in one room | 2 to 6, sometimes 8 | Low, but capped for small groups |
| Murder mystery | Performance and conversation | Usually 8 to 12 | High, everyone holds a role |

Pick an action game if your group wants to move and laugh without putting anyone in the spotlight. Pick an [escape room](/en/activites-insolites/best-escape-games-paris/) if the fun comes mostly from thinking together and your group stays small. Pick a murder mystery if your group enjoys talking, acting up, and no one minds being the centre of attention. For the full range of thrill-seeking formats in the capital, our [unusual activities](/en/activites-insolites/) hub covers the other options.

## When to book

The most requested slots, especially Saturday afternoons and the end of the year, fill up fast because each course only takes a limited number of teams at once. Allow one to two weeks ahead for a small weekday group, and up to three or four weeks for a Saturday or for a large group needing several staggered departures in a row.

If you are organising the activity for a group of more than 20, tell the provider the exact headcount when booking, since that number decides how many departures to plan and the total length of the afternoon.
