You typed "sporty escape room" into a search engine hoping to find a room with a bit more movement than a classic escape room. Bad news, that exact format does not really exist. Good news, two very real formats answer that same craving, and they look nothing alike. Here is which one to pick depending on your group, your budget and how much time you have ahead of you.

## "Sporty escape room" does not mean what you think

A classic escape room locks a team in a single room with puzzles to solve before the clock runs out. Nothing sporty about that, apart from your heart rate climbing as the countdown ticks. "Sporty escape room" is really just a shorthand the public uses to describe two distinct families of activities.

The first is the **action game**, a course of physical and dexterity challenges played indoors, with no final puzzle. The second is the **outdoor city hunt**, an investigation on foot through the streets of a Paris neighbourhood, where walking replaces the locked set. Both earn the word sporty, just not in the same way, and not for the same kind of outing.

## The action game, the indoor version

The action game is the closest match to what most people picture when they search for a sporty escape room. The idea fits in one sentence, your team moves through a series of dexterity, speed and thinking challenges, with no door to unlock and no final puzzle, and a score that decides between teams at the end.

Budget about an hour of play, in teams of 2 to 6, for a price that most often runs between 20 and 45 euros per person depending on the provider and the length you pick. It is also one of the easiest formats to scale for a large group, thanks to staggered starts that let 40, 60 people or more play across the same afternoon.

Bomb Squad, on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd arrondissement, is the Paris reference for this format. The course is played in teams of 2 to 6 over one hour, with staggered starts allowing up to 60 participants across a two-hour window, and the activity is open from age 7 with an accompanying adult. The price drops from 45 euros per player for two down to 28 euros for six. Our full guide to the [action game in Paris](/en/activites-insolites/action-game-paris/) covers how a session actually runs, what differs from one provider to another, and how far ahead to book.

## The outdoor city hunt, the other version

The city hunt answers another part of the same search, the groups who want to move without necessarily being locked in a room. The concept is completely different, the team gets a clue booklet or a route of hints and moves freely on foot through the streets of a neighbourhood, station by station, without a guide accompanying them the whole way.

The format usually runs between two and two and a half hours, much longer than an action game or a classic escape room session, which makes it almost a full outing rather than a simple warm-up before an evening out. The budget tends to stay lighter too, between 15 and 25 euros per person, often with a lower rate for larger groups.

The main strength of the city hunt is that it has no capacity cap set by a single room. A large group simply splits into several independent teams, each following its own booklet, which avoids the headache of booked time slots that a classic escape room or an action game runs into past a certain size. It is also a chance to see a Paris neighbourhood differently than as a tourist, the investigation acting as an excuse for a proper guided-feeling walk.

The format also demands less logistics on the organiser's side. No slot to lock down weeks ahead in a single room, no maximum headcount to respect, each team starts at its own pace on the route it has been handed. That is a real advantage when your final headcount is not settled yet at booking time, a common situation for a bachelorette party or a birthday where confirmations keep trickling in right up to the last moment.

## Comparing the formats

| Format | Location | Duration | Group | Budget per person |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Action game | Indoor set | About 1h | 2 to 6 per team, scalable with staggered starts | €20 to €45 |
| Outdoor city hunt | Streets of a Paris neighbourhood | 2h to 2h30 | A handful of players to several independent teams | €15 to €25 |
| Classic escape room | Indoor set | About 1h | 2 to 6, sometimes 8 | €25 to €35 |

Read this table starting from the duration column. An evening already packed with dinner usually only leaves room for an hour of activity, which naturally points toward the action game or the classic escape room rather than the longer city hunt. A free afternoon, on the other hand, suits the outdoor format very well.

## Which occasion suits which format

Both formats suit roughly the same occasions, just not in the same way.

**For a [bachelor party](/en/evg/) or [bachelorette party](/en/evjf/)**, the action game gets the group moving and adds friendly competition without putting anyone individually on the spot, an advantage over a [murder mystery party](/en/activites-insolites/murder-mystery-party-paris/) where everyone has to hold a role in front of the others. The city hunt suits a plan that starts in the afternoon and ends with dinner or a night out, with the investigation as the main activity before heading somewhere for the rest of the evening.

**For a team building event**, the choice mostly comes down to group size and time available. Our guide to [organising a team building event](/en/team-building-seminaires/team-building-tips/) breaks down budget tiers by team size, a useful reference before picking between the two formats.

**For a birthday**, the action game suits a group after a short, intense experience over lunch or early in the evening, while the city hunt fits better into a full day spent with friends. Our guide on [where to celebrate your birthday in Paris](/en/anniversaires/where-to-celebrate-birthday-paris/) lists other formats sorted by budget and group size.

## When to book

The action game books like a classic escape room, with slots filling up fast on Saturday afternoons and around the end of the year. Allow one to two weeks ahead for a small weekday group, up to three or four weeks for a Saturday or a large group needing several staggered departures.

The outdoor city hunt generally leaves more room, since the format does not depend on a single booked room slot, but it stays exposed to the weather. Check the forecast a few days ahead and plan an indoor backup if the group is large and rain is on the way.

One last thing worth knowing, the season matters far more for the city hunt than for the action game. An outdoor walk in the middle of January holds up much less well than one in September or June, while an air-conditioned room stays the same all year round. If your event falls in the depths of winter, the action game sidesteps that risk while keeping the sporty side of the outing without depending on the sky.
