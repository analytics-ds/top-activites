October in Paris is the month when you dig out your jacket and your last-minute plan falls through, quite literally. The good news is that it is also the richest month of the autumn in covered formats, recurring fixtures and reasons to go out as a group. You just have to get your timing right, because the end of the month is a bottleneck.

## What actually changes in Paris in October

Two things, and they shape everything else.

**The light.** The switch to winter time falls on the last Sunday of October. Overnight, darkness arrives before 6pm, which kills midweek outdoor plans and mechanically pushes afterworks towards covered venues. A rooftop that worked beautifully in September becomes a mid-afternoon plan.

**The weather.** It is not necessarily bad, it is mainly unpredictable, which is worse for a group. Organising an outing for six while leaving the decision to the sky guarantees a forty-message thread and two people dropping out. The reflex that works in October is to book a covered format from the outset and stop thinking about it.

The conclusion is simple, October rewards people who book and punishes people who improvise.

## The fixtures that come back every autumn

October has its classics, the ones that return year after year within a few days. Dates move every season, so check them before blocking out a weekend.

- **The Montmartre harvest festival** takes over the hill for one October weekend, with parades, brass bands and food tastings. Free in the street, packed, and very photogenic.
- **The national science festival** covers the first days of the month, with workshops and open days in labs and museums. The one moment of the year when you get into places that are normally closed to the public.
- **A major contemporary art fair** takes over the Grand Palais in October and spills across the whole city, with satellite shows in the galleries.
- **The Salon du Chocolat** sets up at porte de Versailles at the very end of the month, running into November. Allow half a day and an advanced state of saturation on the way out.

These fixtures work very well for two or four people. Beyond eight, a fair turns into a head-counting exercise, and you are better off with a format that has a time slot and a door.

## What to do when it rains, and it does rain

This is the real question of the month, the one Parisians type in every week. The answer comes in three families.

**Indoor group activities** are the most efficient plan because they settle the weather, the price and the duration in one go. Inside Paris proper, an action game such as Bomb Squad, on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd, runs in teams of 2 to 6 players over one hour, with staggered starts that let up to 60 participants play across a two-hour window. Pricing tapers from 45 euros per player for a pair down to 28 euros for six, and the activity is open from age 7 with an adult.

**Activity bars** add food and drinks on site, which saves the 9pm transfer in the rain that always loses two people on the way. PAN Bar, a shooting bar in the 10th, works in booths of 2 to 10 people from 16 euros per person, up to 66 players across multiple booths, and privatises its 400 sqm space from 30 people. It is closed on Mondays, which matters when you are planning an early-week outing. Our overview of [board game bars in Paris](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/board-game-bars-paris/) covers the other formats in the same register, from board games to darts.

**Museums and exhibitions** remain the simplest fallback for a small group. The October trick is to aim for late afternoon on a weekday, once the school groups have gone, rather than Sunday afternoon, which concentrates everyone.

## Halloween and the autumn holidays, the end of the month that fills up

The last two weeks of October look nothing like the first two.

**Halloween falls on the 31st**, and how saturated it gets depends on a detail nobody checks, the day of the week the date lands on. Midweek, demand spreads over the nearest weekend. On a Friday or a Saturday, everything concentrates into a single night, prices go up and evening slots go three to four weeks ahead. Our dedicated guide to a [Halloween party in Paris](/en/activites-insolites/halloween-party-paris/) breaks down the three families of plans that actually exist that night and what each one costs.

**The autumn school holidays** cover roughly two weeks straddling late October and early November, with the same dates nationwide. In practice, every school-age household is off at the same time, and activities open to minors fill their daytime slots. If you are organising something with teenagers or visiting cousins, this is the fortnight that needs the earliest booking.

**The corporate party season starts**, quietly but surely. Teams that plan well lock in their December date in October, which removes slots from the calendar without it ever showing on consumer platforms.

## October with colleagues, the month when December is decided

For a company, October is not an outing month, it is a booking month. Waiting until November to look for a December date means accepting the slot nobody else wanted.

The format that works in October itself is an afterwork built a little more carefully than usual. A one-hour to ninety-minute activity at the end of the day, then free time at the bar on site, with no travel in between. Three things to check before confirming, the real simultaneous capacity rather than the venue's total capacity, the presence of actual food and not just sharing boards, and how exposed the chosen format leaves people. One person who feels awkward in front of the group is enough to sink the mood.

Our guide to [organising a team building event](/en/team-building-seminaires/team-building-tips/) sets out the budget tiers by team size, and the same logic applies to a seasonal [afterwork](/en/afterwork/).

## The classic October mistakes

- **Keeping an outdoor plan with no fallback.** A picnic, a walk or a terrace in October needs a plan B that is booked, not a plan B that is theoretical.
- **Booking after the 15th for an end-of-month date.** Over that last fortnight, evening slots are already largely absorbed by Halloween and the school holidays.
- **Forgetting the clock change in your timing.** An activity scheduled for 6.30pm on the last weekend of October happens in the dark, which changes the walk from the metro and the feel of the neighbourhood.
- **Confusing total capacity with simultaneous capacity.** A venue advertising 60 people often welcomes them across several starts, not all at once. Ask before you confirm.
