The email that goes round on Thursday afternoon, a bar picked almost at random, and a group that thins out before 9pm for lack of a real plan. After-work drinks in Paris work very well when they are planned ahead of time, and tend to fail for the same avoidable reasons. Here is how to get it right, what it actually costs, and the formats that go beyond the standard drink standing up.

## What an after-work actually is

An after-work is time spent with colleagues right after work, before everyone heads home separately. The classic format starts between 6pm and 7pm, lasts one to two hours, and takes place in a bar close to the office so nobody needs to go out of their way. Some companies organise one at a fixed interval, often once a month, while others leave the initiative to a team that wants to extend a Friday or celebrate a win.

The format usually splits into two stages. A first stage, between 6.30pm and 8pm, fairly calm, good for conversation and for getting to know colleagues you rarely cross paths with day to day. A second stage, after 8pm, livelier if part of the group decides to keep going, often with a change of venue or a step up in energy. Nothing forces you into both, an after-work that wraps up at 8.30pm is still a successful one.

## How much an after-work costs in Paris

The budget mostly depends on the format chosen and the neighbourhood. In a classic bar, budget between 15 and 35 euros per person, drinks and a few shared boards included, with a real gap between a bar in the Opéra district and a venue further out. A group activity such as a shooting bar starts around 16 euros per person for a small booth, with the rate dropping mechanically as the group grows. A board game bar sits in a similar range, often between 10 and 20 euros for entry depending on the venue and the games included.

| Format | Budget per person | Typical length | Book by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic bar, happy hour | €15 to €25 | 1h30 to 2h | Not needed for a small group |
| Rooftop or bar with a view | €20 to €35 | 1h30 to 2h | 1 week, more in summer |
| Board game bar | €10 to €20 entry | 2h | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Shooting bar | From €16 | 1h to 1h30 | 2 to 3 weeks |
| Action game | €28 to €45 | 1h | 3 weeks |

If the company is covering the bill, setting a cap per person in advance avoids surprises at the end of the night, especially at a venue that charges for privatising an area once the headcount passes a certain threshold. ## Which day to pick

Thursday evening remains the most chosen day for an after-work in Paris, a classic that holds up year after year. It comes right before the weekend without Friday's fatigue, which pushes many bars to run happy hours or group deals specifically on that day. Tuesday and Wednesday also work very well for a group that prefers to avoid the crowds, usually with softer prices and quieter venues than at the end of the week.

Friday is still possible, but demand peaks that day, which means booking earlier if the group is larger than about ten people. If your team has some flexibility, asking for a quote on a Thursday rather than a Friday often makes a real difference on the price, especially for a partial privatisation.

## Formats that go beyond the standing drink

The classic after-work trap is going back to the same bar every month until part of the group stops showing up. Varying the format keeps things fresh without complicating the planning, since every option books exactly the same way, an email or a call a few days ahead.

**The shooting bar** turns an after-work into a moment of play without leaving the drink-in-hand format behind. PAN Bar, in the 10th arrondissement, works with booths of 2 to 10 people from 16 euros per person, up to 66 players spread across several booths, and privatises its 400 square metres from 30 people upwards. Closed on Mondays, worth checking before locking in a date.

**The action game** wins over groups who want a real activity before heading to the bar. Bomb Squad, on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd arrondissement, offers one hour of play in teams of 2 to 6 players, with staggered start times that let up to 60 participants go through over a two hour window, at a degressive rate from 45 euros per player for two down to 28 euros for six. Open from age 7, which also makes it an option if the event includes families.

**The board game bar**, meanwhile, keeps the relaxed feel of a classic bar while giving people something to do with their hands, useful for mixed groups who do not yet know each other well. Our overview of [fun bars and venues in Paris](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/) covers the full range of these formats, just as useful for an after-work as for a weekend outing.

## Team after-work or spontaneous after-work

There are two different logics behind an after-work, and they are not planned the same way. The spontaneous after-work comes from a passing impulse, gets decided the same day or the day before, and aims for a nearby bar with no booking. The team after-work, on the other hand, is planned ahead, often involves a whole department, and is worth booking a space or a booth for, to be sure everyone fits in the same venue.

For this second category, it pays to plan it like a real company event. ## The mistakes that sink an after-work

- **Picking a venue too far from the office.** Beyond fifteen minutes of travel, part of the group tends to drop out, especially on a Thursday evening in the middle of a working week.
- **Not giving enough notice.** An email sent the same day leaves no room for people who already have a personal commitment.
- **Forgetting to check the venue's real capacity.** A bar that fills up on a normal Thursday evening gets busy fast, especially on a terrace in summer.
- **Always offering the same format.** After a few months, the repetition tires out even the regulars from day one.

## In short

A successful after-work comes down to three simple habits, pick a venue close by and easy to reach, give the group a few days' notice, and vary the format now and then so it stays a pleasure rather than a routine. The rest is a question of budget and day of the week, with Thursday remaining the safe bet for most Parisian teams.
