Having a drink is fine. Having a drink while playing a board game, shooting at targets or murdering a power ballad in a soundproof room is much better. Paris now has a playful bar for every mood, with prices running from a 3-euro play fee to a 39-euro darts slot. Here are 7 addresses sorted by type of experience, with the real price, the neighbourhood and the group size for each.

## Board game bar or activity bar, which one is for you?

Two very different families hide behind the "playful bar" label, and picking the wrong one is how an evening falls flat.

A **board game bar** works like a normal bar with a self-service game library. You pay a small play fee, you pick from the shelves, you stay three hours. It is the cheapest format, the chattiest, and the best fit for groups that want to talk as much as they play.

An **activity bar** works on booking. You block a lane, a box or a room for an hour or two, with an activity that shapes the whole evening. It costs more, it looks better, and it works far better when the group does not know each other yet.

A simple rule to decide: under 15 euros per person, you are in board game territory. Above that, you are in activity territory. The rest comes down to the vibe you want, and you will find the full picture in our [fun bars and venues](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/) section.

## Board game bars, the small budget that lasts all night

### The Good Game, the biggest game library in the Sentier

Over 800 games playable on site, a shop of around 300 titles to take one home, and a play fee of **3 euros per hour per person**. It is the highest game density per square metre in this list, and probably the best value in Paris for a night out for four.

The bar keeps up with cocktails, local beers, organic wines and enough food to stay put (toasties, sharing boards, homemade desserts). You can spend the whole evening there without ever touching your phone.

**Practical details**: 78 rue de Cléry, 75002 Paris, between Montorgueil and Strasbourg-Saint-Denis. Weekday bookings available.

### Les Grands Gamins, the game bar that can also cook

A few doors away, same street, different priorities. Here the kitchen matters as much as the game library, with sharing boards, mezze and burratas good enough to make you forget you came to play. The library lists over 450 playable games, out of 700 references including the shop.

This is the address to book when half the group wants to play and the other half mainly wants a good dinner. Everyone gets their way and nobody complains at 9pm.

**Practical details**: 19 rue de Cléry, 75002 Paris. Closed Mondays, open until 1am on Fridays and Saturdays. No play fee is listed on their site, so budget mainly for your bill.

### Les Mauvais Joueurs, the simplest deal in Paris

**5 euros per person**, several hundred games in unlimited self-service, one drink to renew every two hours. The price is clear, the rule is clear, and that is exactly the appeal.

The real bonus is the programme: werewolf nights, quizzes, role-playing games. Turn up alone or as a pair and you rarely leave without having played with strangers. Two bars plus a shop, which gives you a plan B when the first one is full.

**Practical details**: 46 rue Sedaine, 75011 Paris (Bastille) and 12 boulevard Vincent Auriol, 75013 Paris (Austerlitz). Closed Mondays, open until 1am on Fridays and Saturdays.

## Bars where the activity is the main event

### Le 3Bis, two bars in one at Bercy Village

Le 3Bis plays both sides, which is what makes it clever. On the game bar side, over 300 board games, arcade cabinets, a table football, darts and a giant wheel, for **3.50 euros per hour per person**.

Next to it, private rooms for karaoke, blind test, quiz, disco club or retro gaming, at **16 euros per hour per person**, and **11 euros off-peak**. Rooms hold 4 to 15 people depending on the theme, which covers just about every group size.

**Practical details**: 41 rue des Pirogues de Bercy, 75012 Paris. Open until 2am Monday to Saturday, and from 10am at weekends for those who play over brunch.

### PAN Bar, clay shooting meets cocktail bar

The concept is simple and there is nothing else like it in Paris. You book a semi-private box, pick up two guns and a tablet, and run through a dozen shooting games while the kitchen sends out tapas and the bar sends out cocktails. All of it in a 500 sqm venue in the 10th.

The format runs 1.5 hours, longer than an escape game and shorter than a full bar night, which makes it a solid backbone for an [afterwork](/en/afterwork/) that has to end before the last metro. From **20 euros per person**, with sessions for 2 to 50 people, up to 150 with a venue buyout.

**Practical details**: 6 rue de Paradis, 75010 Paris. Closed Mondays, open until 1am on Fridays and Saturdays. Online booking, with prices varying by day, time slot and box.

### Les Petites Flèches, darts that keep score for you

Recognition cameras, 12 mini-games and an interface that counts the points, so nobody can round up their score any more. It is the most competitive address here, and the one where groups get the most carried away.

Billing is **per lane and per hour**, with a maximum of 6 players per lane. Expect **29 euros Monday to Thursday**, **39 euros from 7pm on Thursday and at weekends**, and **14 euros on Sundays**, by far the best slot if you are watching the budget. Three Paris venues, so decent odds of one being near you.

**Practical details**: 49 rue Jean-Pierre Timbaud (11th), 7 rue d'Enghien (10th) and 49 boulevard de Clichy (9th). Booking recommended a week ahead.

### BAM Karaoke Box, the classic that never disappoints

45,000 tracks, private rooms and vocal effects that rescue the shakiest singers. Box karaoke has one unbeatable advantage over bar karaoke: nobody outside your group can hear you, so everyone ends up singing, including the one who swore they would not.

From **10 euros per person for 2 hours**, with rooms for 1 to 30 people. Five venues in Paris (Sentier, Madeleine, Richer, Parmentier, Étoile), the densest network in this list. Prices shift with the time slot, and early weekday evenings are the gentlest.

**Practical details**: 5 Paris venues, open until 2am. Online booking strongly recommended at weekends.

## What to actually budget per person

Listed prices are only half the story, the other half is what you drink and eat. Three orders of magnitude for a full evening:

- **Board game night**: 3 to 5 euros of play fee plus two drinks, so around fifteen euros per person. It is the cheapest group night out in Paris, barely more than a drink on a terrace.
- **Weekday activity night**: 15 to 25 euros per person including the activity, provided you fill the lane or the box. Six people on a 29-euro darts lane brings the activity under 5 euros each.
- **Weekend activity night with food**: 40 to 60 euros per person. That is the full evening format, activity plus dinner plus cocktails, without moving venue.

The rule that changes everything: whenever the price is **per lane or per box**, fill it. A 39-euro room split by 6 is a very different story from the same room split by 2.

## Your questions

### What is a game bar?

A regular bar with either a self-service board game library or a built-in activity. Two families coexist in Paris. Board game bars, where you pay a small play fee to access hundreds of boxes, and activity bars, where you book a slot on a lane, a shooting box or a karaoke room.

### How much does a night at a game bar in Paris cost?

Budget around fifteen euros per person in a board game bar, play fee and drinks included. In an activity bar the bill runs from 10 to 39 euros per hour depending on the venue and the day, usually billed per lane or per room, so the cost per head drops as the group grows.

### Can you come as a large group?

Yes, but every venue has its limit. Six players per lane at Les Petites Flèches, 4 to 15 people per room at Le 3Bis, up to 30 in a BAM Karaoke Box room, and 2 to 50 people per session at PAN Bar, which goes up to 150 with a venue buyout.

### Do you need to book?

Always for activity bars, since the number of lanes and rooms is limited. Les Petites Flèches recommend booking a week ahead. For board game bars you can try your luck on a weekday, but call ahead at weekends or as soon as you are 6 or more.

### Which playful bar works for a bachelorette party or a birthday?

A private karaoke room for a group that wants to sing without an audience, a shooting or darts bar for a real activity with food, and a board game bar for a chatty night on a tight budget.
