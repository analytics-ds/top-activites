Karaoke has one virtue, it settles the question of the evening in one go, a private space, an activity and service all in the same place. It also has one flaw, it rests entirely on the willingness to sing in front of others, which leaves part of the group out almost every time. Here are six ways to keep what makes the format work, without the microphone.

## What you are really booking when you book a karaoke

The microphone is not the main reason for the booking. Three things are.

A **private space** first, where the group is at home, with no neighbouring tables and no need to raise your voice. An **activity that gets the evening going** next, because a group sitting around a drink takes time to mix. And **service on site** last, so you do not have to move venue after an hour.

Any credible alternative has to tick those three boxes. That is the filter applied below.

## The comparison at a glance

| Format | Private space | Exposure level | Mood | Food on site |
|--------|---------------|----------------|------|--------------|
| Virtual shooting bar | Closed booth | Low, you play in turns as a team | Active, competitive | Yes |
| Board game bar | Table or lounge | None | Calm, conversation | Depends on venue |
| Connected darts bar | Dedicated lane | Low | Standing, competitive | Often limited |
| Immersive concept bar | Room or space | Varies | Escapist | Depends on venue |
| Classic private lounge | Lounge | None | Free-form | Yes |
| Karaoke | Closed booth | High, you sing alone in front of the group | Festive | Yes |

## The virtual shooting bar, the most direct equivalent

This is the format that most faithfully reuses the way karaoke works, a booth booked by the hour, a built-in activity, ordering from your table.

[PAN Bar](https://pan.bar/), on rue de Paradis in the 10th, opened the city's first virtual shooting bar. The principle comes down to three things, a private booth, two rifles and a tablet used to launch the games and order food and drinks. About ten games were developed in-house, from clay shooting to western settings, which avoids the repetitive effect after an hour.

The comparison with karaoke is direct on one specific point, nobody is exposed alone in front of the group. You shoot in turns, scores come up on screen, and people who have never held a rifle find their feet by the second round. That is what makes it a more consensual format for a mixed group.

On packages, the simple one starts at 16 euros per person for booths of 2 to 66 people, the [team building package](https://pan.bar/team-building/) at 36 euros with a drink included, and a full buyout is quoted case by case. The venue is also a restaurant, with homemade cooking and a cocktail list, so the whole evening can run without moving. Closed on Mondays.

## The board game bar, to talk as much as play

At the other end of the spectrum, the board game bar goes for relative calm and conversation. [Le 3Bis](https://le3bis.com/) works on that model, with a library of games and spaces that are more or less separate depending on how busy it is.

This format suits groups of 4 to 10 who want a long evening without noise pressure. It shows its limit beyond that, because a board game does not gather fifteen people around one table. Our other [board game bars in Paris](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/board-game-bars-paris/) detail the capacity of each venue.

## Connected darts and competitive formats

Electronic darts bars have grown in recent years. They reuse the mechanic that makes karaoke work, everyone takes a turn while the others watch, but with a score at stake rather than a vocal performance. The sorting happens on capacity per lane, often capped at five or six players, which forces you to book several lanes for a large group.

## Private lounges with no activity

Some bars and restaurants rent out a closed lounge with no set activity. It is the most flexible option and often the cheapest per person, but it asks the group to create its own dynamic. Book it when the group already knows each other well, otherwise the evening stays frozen in small circles.

## And if you keep the karaoke

It remains relevant in one specific case, a group that knows each other, where everyone is willing. [BAM Karaoke Box](https://fr.bam-karaokebox.com/paris/) is still the Paris reference on that format, with private rooms in various sizes.

If you are hesitating, the right reflex is to ask the group plainly before booking. One reluctant person is enough to unbalance a karaoke evening, while they would go unnoticed in a team format.

## How to choose by occasion

- **Team afterwork**: an active, short format, shooting bar or darts, with food on site. See also our [afterwork in Paris](/en/afterwork/) ideas.
- **Birthday with friends**: a private booth, so the place is yours and you can actually talk.
- **Stag or hen party**: a competitive format that gets everyone on their feet, covered in our [stag](/en/evg/) and [hen party](/en/evjf/) selections.
- **Team evening with very mixed profiles**: avoid anything that exposes one person alone in front of the others.

## The two questions to ask before booking

First, is the space genuinely closed or is it a corner of a shared room. The difference changes everything about noise level and how freely the group behaves.

Second, does the package include a drink. That is where the gap opens between two prices that look similar, and it is the number one source of a bad surprise on the final bill.
