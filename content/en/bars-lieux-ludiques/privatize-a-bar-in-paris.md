A birthday, a bachelorette party, a farewell drink or an afterwork that runs long, at some point a simple corner of a bar is not enough anymore. Privatizing a bar in Paris solves the problem in one move, no outside customers, an atmosphere that stays the group's own and music you get to choose. You still need to know how much it really costs, from how many people it makes sense, and which mistakes make you pay too much for nothing.

## Booking or privatizing, the real difference

Many groups mix up the two words, even though the difference completely changes the budget. A regular booking blocks a few tables or a section of the bar, while the rest of the customers keep moving around you. This is free or low cost in most Paris venues, with the only condition being a minimum number of covers past a certain headcount.

Privatization, on the other hand, closes the entire venue or a dedicated room, mezzanine, basement or vaulted cellar, exclusively for your group. The manager gives up regular business for that time slot, which is why they almost always ask for a minimum headcount and a guaranteed package. This is the format that makes sense for a **bachelor party**, a **bachelorette party** or a birthday that wants its own atmosphere without outside eyes, two occasions covered in our guides to [bachelor party activities in Paris](/en/evg/bachelor-party-paris/) and [bachelorette parties in Paris](/en/evjf/bachelorette-party-paris/).

## How much it really costs

Budget varies a lot from one neighborhood to another, but a realistic range sits between 20 and 60 euros per person, drinks and sometimes light food included. A full privatization package generally starts around 500 euros for an evening of a few hours in a small venue, and climbs quickly with floor space, number of hours and the day chosen.

Three variables move the bill. The **neighborhood** first, a venue in Le Marais or Saint-Germain always charges more than a bar in the 19th or 20th arrondissement for an equivalent floor space. The **day of the week** next, an event organized Monday through Thursday costs 30 to 50 percent less than a comparable Friday or Saturday. The **season** finally, December and the months before summer show higher rates than January or February, due to lower demand.

Beyond the base package, always check three lines on the quote, the guaranteed minimum spend even if the group drinks little, a possible cover charge per person, and the security deposit requested to secure the booking.

## From how many people it is worth it

The break even point changes depending on venue size and the type of privatization sought. A small room, mezzanine or cellar, is often privatized from around fifteen people, because the space involved stays limited and easy to isolate from the rest of the venue. An entire ground floor generally needs a larger headcount, around twenty five to thirty people depending on the venue, for the arrangement to stay worthwhile for the manager against the lost regular business.

Below this threshold, booking several tables often stays cheaper than a full privatization, especially for a group of ten to fifteen people who do not need to exclude everyone else. Our guide to [alternatives to a classic bar in Paris](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/alternative-to-a-classic-bar-paris/) covers other formats, board game bars, shooting bars or karaoke boxes, which offer privatization by box or by lane, accessible from two to six people without needing to fill an entire ground floor.

The average age of the group also plays a role many forget to mention to the manager. A group in their twenties planning a dance heavy evening does not have the same needs as a group of colleagues in their forties who mainly want to chat quietly, and some venues shape their answer around this criterion as much as around headcount. Be upfront from the first contact about the atmosphere you are after, it avoids discovering on the spot a venue with a sound system that is too loud, or on the contrary too muted for the group's energy.

## The right format for the occasion

The choice between booking, partial privatization and full privatization mainly depends on the occasion and group size. Here are the benchmarks that come up most often.

| Occasion | Typical group size | Recommended format | Indicative budget |

| Afterwork with colleagues | 8 to 20 people | Table booking | 20 to 30 euros per person |
| Birthday with friends | 15 to 30 people | Partial privatization | 30 to 45 euros per person |

## How to book without a bad surprise

The most efficient approach is to contact two or three venues directly that match your neighborhood and budget, stating upfront the number of people, the desired date and the budget per head. Matchmaking platforms such as Privateaser or Reserverunbar also let you compare several available venues on the same date without contacting each one individually, a real time saver once the date is set a few weeks out.

Plan at least two to three weeks ahead for a Saturday night in peak season, and up to a month for a group of more than thirty people in December. Before paying a deposit, get written confirmation of the total package, the guaranteed minimum spend, the exact start and end time, and the cancellation terms. An activity venue such as [PAN Bar](https://pan.bar/), in the 10th arrondissement, offers for example semi private shooting boxes for 2 to 10 people from 16 euros per person, with full privatization of its 400 sqm available from 30 people, closed on Mondays. That is the kind of detail worth checking systematically before fixing a date.

Always ask for at least two quotes before deciding, even when the first venue contacted seems to fit. Price gaps between two comparable venues in the same neighborhood can reach 20 to 30 percent depending on the time of year and how full the manager's calendar usually runs for that slot. Comparing in writing also helps spot hidden extra fees that do not always come up on the phone, a sound system surcharge, a coat check fee or a markup past a certain hour.

## The mistakes that cost you

- **Not asking about the minimum spend before signing.** It is often the line that inflates the final bill the most, especially if the group drinks less than expected.
- **Picking a Saturday by default.** A Thursday or a Sunday costs 30 to 50 percent less for an atmosphere that is often just as good with a group that privatizes the venue.
- **Underestimating the booking lead time.** The best December and year end slots sometimes get booked a month ahead, a last minute afterwork quickly gets limited to a non privatized ground floor.
- **Forgetting to check the closing day.** Many activity bars close on Mondays, a detail that is costly to discover after fixing the date with twenty people.
- **Not confirming in writing.** A package quoted over the phone that changes on arrival is the number one source of disputes, always ask for an email confirmation before paying the deposit.
