Planning a friend's bachelor party comes down to three unknowns at once, the budget the group can actually put together, the activity that will land without putting the less sporty guys on the spot, and the timing so the best Saturday slots aren't already taken by another group. Paris has the advantage of packing a huge range of activities within half an hour of each other, which makes the logistics of a multi-part day much simpler. Here is how to set the budget, pick the right format for the vibe you want, and time the calendar so the groom only has to sit back and be surprised.

## How much does a bachelor party cost in Paris

The budget varies hugely depending on whether the day stays in Paris or turns into a weekend with accommodation. For a single-day format, budget between 50 and 150 euros per participant, activity, meal and small extras included. For a weekend with an overnight stay, the range climbs to between 250 and 500 euros per person, with lodging accounting for a good part of the gap depending on the standard chosen.

| Format | Budget per person | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| Single activity | 20 to 50 euros | One activity, meal not included |
| Full day in Paris | 50 to 150 euros | Activity, meal, evening out |
| Weekend with accommodation | 250 to 500 euros | Lodging, activities, meals |

The rule that comes up most often among best men who organise these, the groom pays nothing or almost nothing, and the rest of the group splits the bill evenly. Sharing the total budget from the invitation onward avoids nasty surprises and last-minute drop-outs, especially once the group passes ten people.

## Which activity to pick depending on the vibe you want

Picking the right activity depends less on the current trend than on the group's real personality. One question almost always settles it, does the group prefer moving around or thinking together, and is everyone comfortable being watched or would they rather stay blended into a team.

**For a group that wants to move and laugh without putting anyone on the spot**, an [action game like the one at Bomb Squad](/en/activites-insolites/action-game-paris/) ticks every box. Teams of 2 to 6 players run through a course of challenges over an hour, on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd arrondissement, a format that works just as well for 6 friends as for a larger group of 20 thanks to staggered starts, with a sliding price from 45 euros per player for two down to 28 euros for six.

**For a competition that needs no physical fitness at all**, a shooting bar plays on the same register of adrenaline while staying seated, with food and drinks on site, which avoids the 9pm move that always loses someone along the way.

**For something more original that photographs well**, [axe throwing in Paris](/en/activites-insolites/axe-throwing-paris/) runs in one-hour sessions between 19 and 27 euros per person, with a coach who teaches the technique in a few minutes, a good option for a group of 6 to 30 people.

**For a group that enjoys thinking together**, an [escape room](/en/activites-insolites/best-escape-games-paris/) remains a classic that works, as long as you keep each room to 2 or 6 players and book several rooms in parallel for a larger group.

## Other classic formats that always land well

Beyond the thrill-seeking activities available inside Paris, several formats come up again and again in bachelor party plans, usually just outside the city or in the near suburbs and worth booking several weeks ahead.

**Paintball and laser tag** remain safe bets for groups that want to burn energy as a team, in venues often located outside Paris, worth checking at booking time for the number of players and session length.

**Karting** works well for a group that enjoys individual, timed competition, with a leaderboard at the end that crowns a winner for the day, a good excuse for a small forfeit.

**A zip-line adventure course or a thrill-seeking day trip** suits groups who want to leave Paris for the day and turn the bachelor party into a real outing, as long as everyone accepts the travel time and checks the weather before locking in the date.

For these out-of-town formats, prices and group sizes vary a lot from one operator to another, so the safer move is to compare several quotes rather than trust a single price posted online.

## Where to go for drinks after the activity

Once the activity wraps up, two neighbourhoods hold the highest concentration of bars and lively venues suited to a large group that wants to hop between several addresses. **Le Marais**, with its bars and vaulted cellars, suits a group happy to walk from one address to the next over the course of the evening. **Oberkampf** leans more festive, with a cluster of bars and nightlife spots just a few minutes' walk from each other.

For a group that would rather not move once settled, our overview of [bars and games venues in Paris](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/) lists addresses that combine an activity, a meal and a bar under one roof, which avoids exactly that late-evening scattering of the group.

## Who organises it and when to book

By tradition, the best man takes charge of the organising, alone or with a few close friends if several people are especially invested. The programme is built around what will please the groom, not around the tastes of whoever is organising.

On timing, start planning two to four months before the wedding, enough time to check availability and settle on a date that works for the most people. For the activities themselves, book three to four weeks ahead in normal periods, and up to six to eight weeks if the party falls between May and September, Paris's peak season, when the best Saturday slots go first.

## Two verified venues to round out the programme

Two Paris venues cover the two most requested vibes for a bachelor party well, pure action on one side, seated competition on the other.

[Bomb Squad](/en/activites-insolites/action-game-paris/), on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd arrondissement, runs a one-hour action game course in teams of 2 to 6 players, open from age 7 with an adult. The sliding price drops from 45 euros per player for two down to 28 euros for six, and staggered starts let up to 60 people play in the same afternoon, useful if the party brings together several friend circles who don't all know each other.

PAN Bar, in the 10th arrondissement, works as a virtual shooting bar starting at 16 euros per person, in booths of 2 to 10 players. For a larger group, several booths can be booked in parallel for up to 66 players, and a full buyout of the venue's 400 square metres starts at 30 people. The bar is closed on Mondays, worth keeping in mind if the party falls early in the week.
