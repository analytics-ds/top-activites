Organising the end of year party is usually the file that lands on a corner of a desk in October, with a vague budget and a date nobody has blocked yet. Except that in Paris, December is the tightest month of the year for anything that can be privately hired. Here is what you need to settle, in the order it actually comes up, so you do not end up with a Tuesday 8 December in a room with no windows.

## Start with the date, not the venue

It feels backwards, but looking for a venue before the date is fixed costs you three weeks. As long as management has not signed off on the date, every option you find expires before it can be confirmed, and you start again.

In December, demand concentrates on a handful of evenings. The Thursdays and Fridays of the first two weeks go first, followed by the same slots in the third week. After the 20th, attendance collapses as people leave on holiday. The result is that around ten dates absorb most of the Paris demand, and prices follow.

Two trade-offs genuinely take the pressure off:

- **Moving the party midweek**, to a Tuesday or a Wednesday, frees up availability and often brings the private hire price down.
- **Pushing it into January**, as a new year party rather than an end of year one, opens a completely relaxed market, with more available teams and suppliers coming out of their peak.

The second is a real and underused lever. A party on 15 January gathers more people than a party on 18 December, quite simply because personal calendars are not saturated yet.

## Budget per person, the three tiers of the Paris market

Always work the budget out per guest, never as a total envelope, otherwise you cannot compare a single quote. Three tiers structure what Paris offers.

| Format | Budget per person | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Afterwork or light cocktail | 20 to 40 € | Drinks and canapés, over a 2 to 3 hour slot |
| Dinner with a private room | 55 to 95 € | Seated meal or buffet, reserved space, drinks package |
| Full private-hire evening | 95 to 160 € | Whole venue, catering, drinks, entertainment and tech |

The range most often observed sits around 80 to 150 euros per guest for an evening that really holds together. Below 50 euros you are doing an upgraded drinks reception, which is perfectly respectable as long as you announce it as such.

Three line items organisers systematically leave out of their first estimate:

- **The cloakroom**, billed separately by many Paris venues in December.
- **Running over time**, often charged by the quarter hour beyond the contractual end time.
- **Extra staff**, as soon as you go past the venue's standard capacity.

On this point, the same logic as [organising a team building](/en/team-building-seminaires/team-building-tips/) applies. A venue that charges by space, by box or by room rather than per head makes the cost per person drop as soon as you fill it.

## Which format for which team

There is no good format in the abstract, there is a format that matches your headcount and what the year has been like.

| Format | Suitable headcount | For what kind of year |
|---|---|---|
| Extended afterwork | 10 to 30 | A team that already sees plenty of each other |
| Activity then meal | 15 to 60 | A team that has been hiring, where not everyone knows everyone |
| Seated dinner with a speech | 30 to 150 | A year worth celebrating, with results to announce |
| Private party with dancing | 60 and above | A hard year, where the point is mainly to let go |

The activity then meal format is the most forgiving. It fills the first hour, the one where nobody knows what to say, and it gives everyone a shared topic for the rest of the evening. It is also the one that works best with a mixed group, because it does not force anyone to make conversation from a standing start.

Two concrete Paris examples, with their real constraints. Bomb Squad, an action game venue on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd arrondissement, runs teams of 2 to 6 players over one hour, with staggered start times that allow up to 60 participants across two hours, on a sliding rate from 45 euros per player for two down to 28 euros for six. PAN Bar, a shooting bar in the 10th, starts at 16 euros per person with boxes of 2 to 10 players, goes up to 66 players across multiple boxes and privatises its 400 sq m from 30 people onwards, bearing in mind that it is closed on Mondays.

Other evening formats are laid out in our comparison of [alternatives to a classic bar in Paris](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/alternative-to-a-classic-bar-paris/), which works just as well for a professional group.

## Entertainment, what separates a party from a meal

A company party without entertainment is a dinner where everyone sits with the three people they already bump into at the coffee machine every day. Entertainment is not there to amuse, it is there to break up the tables.

What works, in order of effectiveness:

- **A collective activity to open the evening**, with teams deliberately reshuffled across departments. It is the only setup that mechanically produces new conversation.
- **A short awards moment**, five minutes maximum, naming specific people rather than departments.
- **A light running thread** across the evening, a photo, a quiz or a collective challenge, giving people a reason to move between tables.

What works less well than people think is the entertainment supplier who occupies the stage while the room watches. It fills the time, it creates no connection.

Watch out too for the compulsory fancy dress trap. A themed party works when the theme is a suggestion, not a condition of entry. Otherwise part of the team pulls out the day before.

## The timeline, from September to the day itself

The Paris timeline for December comes down to five milestones, and the first one has already passed if you are reading this in November.

| Deadline | What is at stake |
|---|---|
| September | Date signed off internally, budget per person agreed |
| D-75, mid-October | Venue booked and deposit paid |
| D-60, early November | Caterer, entertainment and tech selected |
| D-45, mid-November | Invitations sent, dietary requirements collected |
| D-10 | Final headcount confirmed to venue and caterer |

Three mistakes come back every year:

- **Announcing the date before paying the deposit.** An option is not a booking, and a Paris venue in December will not hold one for more than ten days.
- **Counting the theoretical headcount.** Out of 80 invitations, between holidays, travel and drop-outs, you rarely host more than 65 people. Planning exactly 80 covers means paying for 80 and serving 65.
- **Forgetting the end of the evening.** A party that finishes at 1am on the outskirts ends in taxis, and the transport budget was nowhere in the quote.

## Legal and social basics to keep in mind

Three points are enough to keep the organisation safe, and none of them are complicated.

Attendance is not compulsory. An invited employee can decline without giving a reason, especially if the party is held outside working hours. The employer, however, has to invite every employee within the scope chosen, otherwise leaving some out can be contested.

The meal provided counts as a benefit in kind, with the social contributions that go with it. Gifts handed out on the occasion benefit from a French social security exemption tolerance, within a ceiling per employee and per event that is revalued every year and sits around 200 euros. Check the exact figure for the current year before settling your gift budget, it is the kind of threshold that moves.

One last point, the one always forgotten. The employer's duty of care does not stop at the venue door. Providing genuinely appealing non-alcoholic drinks, not just bottles of water, and planning how people get home remains the simplest way to do this properly.
