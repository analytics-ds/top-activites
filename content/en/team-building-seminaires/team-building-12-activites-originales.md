Organising a team building in Paris means choosing between two moods: getting active together, or sharing a more relaxed moment. The right pick depends on group size, the goal (cohesion, reward, onboarding) and the atmosphere. Here is an overview of the formats that come up most often, with cues to decide.

## Choosing the right team building activity

Three questions before booking: how many participants, what goal, and what mood. A one-hour activity is often enough to get the dynamic going, before extending around a meal. For a team with varied profiles, a format accessible to everyone avoids leaving people out.

Needs also differ by occasion: a [company seminar](/en/team-building-seminaires/) does not call for the same flow as a team afterwork or an end-of-year outing.

## Activities to get moving

Sporty, playful formats work well when the team wants to let off steam and laugh together.

- **Bomb Squad** offers an action game halfway between laser game and escape game, without puzzles: rooms in a row where the group takes on challenges to score high and defuse a paint bomb. Very visual and physical, it commonly hosts up to 40 to 50 people, at around 25 to 30 euros per participant for a roughly one-hour activity.
- **Laser game** and **axe throwing** are other thrill options offered by several venues in Paris, creating friendly competition.

These belong to the world of [insolite and thrill activities](/en/activites-insolites/).

## Puzzle-based activities

For teams that enjoy cooperating under pressure, puzzle games remain a safe bet.

- **The Game** is one of the best-known escape games in Paris, with several themed rooms. Best in small groups spread across rooms, then reunited for a friendly debrief.
- Urban treasure hunts offer an outdoor alternative that blends discovery and collaboration.

## Convivial activities

When the goal is to talk, drink and eat together, a leisure venue with catering takes over.

- **PAN Bar** combines three elements in one place: an activity inspired by clay shooting (a box, tablet-based games), homemade catering and a cocktail bar. The format suits team evenings that last and can host up to 150 people.
- Social bars such as **BAM Karaoké Box** (private rooms) or **le 3bis** (games bar) play the same card: conviviality and play in one spot.

These venues belong to the world of [bars and leisure venues](/en/bars-lieux-ludiques/).

## How to choose

- **Mixed team, moderate budget**: an action game or laser game, a good balance of fun and cohesion.
- **Dynamic group**: a thrill activity or an urban treasure hunt.
- **Convivial goal**: a leisure bar with catering, to extend the evening.

## Practical notes

- Most activities start from 6 to 8 participants.
- Factor in travel and a break on top of the activity itself.
- Weekday evening slots, in high demand, book up early, especially at year end.
